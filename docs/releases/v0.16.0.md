# v0.16.0 specimen section images

This release adds two imaging layers of the Digimouse specimen to the registered-volume reference. A virtual section can now show the source colour cryosection photograph or the source X-ray CT on its cut face, at the position of the cut and on the same grid as the source labels. No anatomical surface, source identifier or coordinate frame changed. [Section image methods](../section-images.md) record the sources, packaging, checks and limits.

## Implemented behavior

- **Cut face** in Display controls chooses the specimen photograph, CT or none for a section of the registered-volume reference. **Cut through the specimen** in Layers opens a central section along display Y, opens the section controls and turns the camera to face the cut. **Open specimen photographs** leads there from the lightweight reference, whose tetrahedral frame has no accepted relation to the image grid. Other references have no section images.
- Images load only on request. They are bound to checksums, expanded and downsampled by two in plane in a worker, and masked to the labelled body with the source label image, so that the surrounding embedding medium is not shown. The image choice is not written to view files and is unavailable in Compare.
- A caption states what the cut face shows, including that the photograph is of frozen postmortem tissue.
- Regions behind the opaque, uncut body surface are no longer drawn or picked. On the 0.4 mm grid a thin body surface can fall between samples, which placed patches of masseter muscle, liver and skeleton in view on the skin, although in the 0.1 mm source only the residual tissue label meets the background. The gaps themselves remain as small dents, because closing them would add geometry. See [viewer presentation](../viewer-presentation.md).

## Executed verification

423 JavaScript and simulated DOM cases passed with no failures or skips, including new cases for the manifest and its rejected alterations, the actual streams against their checksums, the JPEG segments of all 208 slices, the voxel-centre coordinate convention, downsampling and masking, and the interface controls. TypeScript, dependency notices and the production build passed. The Python publication, registered-anatomy, launcher, release, distribution, resource and geometry suites passed, including a new suite that rejects JPEG metadata segments and altered streams and reproduces the three streams and the manifest from the source archives with identical bytes.

Decoded slice luminance correlates with the separately distributed greyscale cryostat volume at 0.9988 to 0.9997 in the identity orientation, against at most 0.8582 for either flip. Mean CT values of 4.1 in the background, 117.8 in the residual tissue label and 223.1 in the skeleton label show that the CT and labels share the grid. Mean photograph luminance, on the 0 to 255 scale, is 15 in the heart label and 31 in the liver label against 87 in the residual tissue label, so that the dark tissue falls inside the labels drawn for it. These are consistency checks and do not measure registration accuracy.

A headless Chrome session with software WebGL rendered the photograph and CT on display X, Y and Z sections, reached from the lightweight reference, without console errors. In the screenshots the images meet the surrounding skin surface and the bright CT structures resemble the spine, ribs and skull. With opaque skin the earlier patches of inner regions were absent, and the inner regions returned with the skin at 30%, on a section with no image and with Reveal organs. This is visual inspection of software rendering, not a measurement of registration accuracy, representative GPU performance or anatomical review.

## Scientific and operational limits

The photographs show a frozen postmortem specimen at the block face, not living tissue, and blood-rich organs appear nearly black. CT values are 8-bit source values without Hounsfield calibration. Coregistration is the source authors' and is not independently verified. The images display at 0.2 mm in plane and add no structure names. An exact replica of a living mouse remains outside what the available sources support: whole-body vessels, nerves, most muscles and glands are absent from the bundled whole-body references, and the specimens are hairless. Keyboard and assistive-technology review, representative hardware measurement and independent anatomical review remain outstanding.
