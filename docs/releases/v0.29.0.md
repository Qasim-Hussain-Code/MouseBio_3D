# v0.29.0 zooming into each tissue

This release extends the [zoom from skin to the proton](../scale-journey.md). The reference models are unchanged.

## Implemented behavior

- The zoom can now start from muscle, nerve, artery, vein, bone, fat, liver, kidney, lung, small intestine, heart or brain, as well as skin. Each has two new simulated levels, a block about 1.6 mm across and a view of its cells about 70 micrometres across, and then joins the shared levels at the cell nucleus.
- **Zoom in** in the bar for the selected structure starts from the tissue of that structure. With [all layers together](../all-layers.md) open, zooming right on to a structure in the middle of the view does the same.
- A notice names the structure and says that the view is a representative example of its tissue and not that part of this mouse. Structures with no route say so, and no route of another tissue stands in for them.

## Corrections

- With all layers together open, each report of what could be seen caused the view to be looked at again, without end, which also made the names flicker. A new look now happens only when the view or the settings have changed.
- A place chosen by name keeps that name in the list. The neck was called the upper fore limb, and the abdomen the chest wall, after their nearest bones.
- The abdomen now opens over the intestines, where it had opened mostly over the liver.
- Inner structures are now drawn only where the view passes through the opening. Long triangles of a few muscles near the knee could otherwise be seen standing through the coat beside an opening, although their vertices lay under the skin.

## Other changes

- Names stand beside the animal as soon as all layers together is opened, and **Names on the mouse** turns them off.
- A click on a paw, the tail or an ear now opens the layers there, as a click on the coat does, with an opening no wider than the part.

## Limits

These levels are simulated and were not seen in this mouse. Positions and counts are generated, and sizes are estimates except where a caption gives a measure and its source. Peltonen et al. describe the human nerve. Brown fat, the colon, the caecum, the stomach, the spleen, the bladder and the glands have no route of their own. All routes join a nucleus level drawn for a keratinocyte. None of this has been reviewed by a histologist.
