# v0.28.0 all layers together, and named vessels and nerves

This release continues the [living mouse model](../living-mouse.md), which now holds 374 structures: 36 source-labelled, 152 traced and 186 simulated. The source references are unchanged.

## Implemented behavior

- [All layers together](../all-layers.md) shows all the layers of the mouse at the same time. At one place each layer is cut back a little less than the layer above it, and vessels and nerves are not cut. A slider unfolds the layers from the outside inwards. The whole side, head, neck, chest, abdomen, hip, shoulder, fore limb, thigh and shank can be opened directly, and a click on the outside of the mouse opens it at that point.
- A list says what lies at the place, from the outside inwards, with the evidence mark of each structure. Pointing at the view names what is under the pointer, a click selects it, and names can stand in columns beside the animal with lines to the parts in view. Which structures are in view is recorded by drawing each off screen in its own flat colour.
- The skin can be folded back about the top of the opening, and the organs can be cut into.

## Named vessels and nerves

Vessels, nerves and lymph nodes that were held in groups are now separate structures. The groups of limb arteries, veins and nerves became named structures such as the femoral, saphenous, popliteal and deep femoral arteries, the sciatic, tibial, common peroneal, sural, median, ulnar and radial nerves, and the lateral saphenous and ischial veins. Groups of the head and trunk became the common, internal and external carotid arteries, the jugular veins, the vagus and phrenic nerves, each cranial nerve, the cervical, thoracic, lumbar and sacral nerves, the arteries and veins of the abdomen, and lymph nodes at eleven sites.

The arteries of the hind limb were redrawn. They now follow the branching and relations in the dissections of Kochi et al. (2013) in the mouse, in place of one route down the limb. The sciatic nerve now rises from the third and fourth lumbar nerves after Rigaud et al. (2008), and divides into three branches after Shields et al. (2003). These structures remain simulated: courses and radii are estimates placed on the measured joints, and other nerves and the fore limb follow Greene (1935), which describes the rat. The arteries of the forearm are left undivided, because no source for the mouse was checked.

## Corrections

Release v0.27.0 recorded that traced limb muscles and some bones reached beyond the simulated limb skin. The limb skin now follows the centre line of the measured bones in the stance, and it is wider where they need it. Traced muscles of the shanks and forearms are drawn in under the skin, and any other soft part that the posture leaves outside the skin is moved to just under it. Bones are not moved. Compared on vertices with the skin in the standing pose, the soleus, which reached about 2.8 mm beyond it, no longer does so, and the small intestine and thigh muscles no longer stand out. The right fibula still reaches about 0.6 mm at most, near the knee, where the comparison with the nearest skin vertex is least exact. Parts in the paws, tail and eyes are closed by their own surfaces and were not compared.

## Limits

All layers together is a display of the model and not a dissection of an animal. The steps and folds are chosen for viewing, and the order of the layers is a display choice. Where a surface is opened, the darker inner side is the inside of a hollow surface, and it is not a section through tissue. None of this has been reviewed by an anatomist.
