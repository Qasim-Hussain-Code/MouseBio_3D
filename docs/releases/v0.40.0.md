# v0.40.0 sources and evidence, a running suture and the named vessels of the neck and limbs

A page behind the information button now says, for every structure, level and procedure, what it rests on. The model gains forty-one named vessels and nerves, three procedures are added at the bench, and two kinds of small structure that showed through the skin on a close look now lie under it.

## Implemented behavior

- **Sources and evidence:** the information button in the header opens a page that says how many structures of the open reference are source-labelled, traced and simulated, and lists every structure with its kind of evidence, its description and the works it follows. Each work is listed with a link, with the structures and levels of the zoom that rest on it, and with a note where citing it alone would claim more than was done: the description of the rat by Greene (1935) says that its text could not be read. Every level of the zoom below the layers is listed with its kind of knowledge and its basis, and every procedure of the bench with its sources. The page also lists what is illustrative and what has not been checked. Lists can be searched and narrowed by kind of evidence. The working views are unchanged.
- **Named vessels and nerves:** forty-one simulated structures are added. From the plates of the circulatory system in Cook (1965), chiefly figures 79 to 85, 91, 97, 98, 100, 101 and 106 to 108: the cervical and costocervical trunks, the thoraco-acromial, lateral thoracic, subscapular, profunda brachii, ulnar collateral, radial and median arteries, the axillary, lateral thoracic, basilic and median veins, the anterior and posterior tibial and sural arteries, the vessels of the liver, stomach, spleen, pancreas, intestine, adrenal glands and bladder, the hypogastric, testicular and inferior phrenic veins, and the anterior jugular, facial and superficial temporal vessels of the head, with the cephalic vein joined to the external jugular vein. The vessels of the paws follow figures 103 to 105 and 108: the marginal, dorsal metatarsal and medial tarsal vessels of the hind foot, the veins to its pads, and the dorsal metacarpal arteries of the fore paw. The forearm arteries, brachial veins and tibial arteries, which had been held as single courses, are replaced by their named branches. The recurrent laryngeal nerves and the coeliac ganglia are added.
- **Nerves on mouse sources:** the twelve cranial nerves now follow the reconstruction of the mouse at 14.5 days of gestation by Reissig et al. 2022 ([doi:10.3389/fcell.2022.1006620](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2022.1006620)), placed in the adult head. Each spinal nerve and its ganglion lies at the end of its measured vertebra, after Sleigh et al. 2020 ([doi:10.1186/s13104-020-05147-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-020-05147-6)), the fourth lumbar ganglion largest. The sympathetic trunk begins at the superior cervical ganglion at the bifurcation of the carotid artery (Ning et al. 2022, [doi:10.1523/ENEURO.0093-22.2022](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0093-22.2022)), with the stellate ganglion between the seventh cervical and first thoracic vertebrae (Duan et al. 2024, [doi:10.1186/s40001-024-01815-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40001-024-01815-6)), and the roots of the brachial plexus come from C5 to T1 (Zhong et al. 2022, [doi:10.1186/s12974-022-02632-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12974-022-02632-6)). The vagus nerve, the facial nerve, the median nerve, the middle ear and the ventricles of the brain also rest on work in the mouse. The structures that cite the description of the rat fall from 79 to 44.
- **Under the skin:** a close look at the head found the glands of the eyelids showing as pale stripes round the eye and the follicles of the whiskers as pink rods along the lip, while the zoom had the layers open. The glands now lie in the lids, under the skin, and each whisker grows from the skin of the snout with its follicle beneath it.
- **Clippers, scrubbing and a running suture:** clippers clear the coat where they pass, leaving the skin bare within, as a depilatory would, and the hair cut short at the edge. A swab with no blood to clean scrubs the skin. The needle and suture now close every layer that lies open along a cut, from the skin to the lining, with a running suture of slanted loops 1 mm apart, a seam and a knot at each end, after Carlson and Chakkalakal 2011 ([doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0024212](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0024212)). The loops are drawn on the skin once the edges have come together.
- **Open and close the abdomen:** a tutorial after Carlson and Chakkalakal 2011 and the guide to rodent surgery of Pritchett-Corning et al. 2011 ([doi:10.3791/2586](https://doi.org/10.3791/2586)): the hair is clipped, the skin scrubbed from the middle outwards, a paper drape with an opening laid over the animal, a cut of about 2 cm made down the midline from just below the breastbone, first in the skin and then in the wall, the organs looked at, and the whole closed with one running suture.
- **Draw blood from the heart:** a tutorial after Parasuraman et al. 2010 ([doi:10.4103/0976-500X.72350](https://doi.org/10.4103/0976-500X.72350)), with the chest opened first after Gage et al. 2012. The needle goes for the cavity of the left ventricle, and the syringe fills slowly with dark blood while it is held there. The step counts once it is at least four fifths full.
- **Draw blood from the saphenous vein:** a tutorial after the same account. The animal stands, the hair is clipped over the lateral saphenous vein of the left shank, the needle goes in shallow along the vein and stops in it, a drop of blood comes up where it came out, and a swab presses on the place. This and drawing blood from the heart form a new group, blood sampling.
- **Lifting out the brain:** exposing the brain now ends with lifting it out. It comes away whole, with its parts and the arteries on its surface.
- **The coat:** seen from far away, where one hair is narrower than a pixel, the coat takes the mean colour of its hairs instead of flickering, and lies a little flatter. The pale hair of the belly is shorter and lies close. At the bench the coat is cleared from a narrow band beside the fold of a reflected flap, where hairs stood up through the fold. White fat is drawn paler and the gall bladder darker.

## Limits

- The named vessels follow plates of one dissection, drawn in two dimensions, and were carried into the stance by estimate. They are simulated, and no vessel of this specimen was traced for them.
- The cranial nerves follow a description of the fetus. Their courses in the adult head are estimates.
- The suture is a drawing. Nothing holds the edges together but the drawing, and the thread is not modelled.
- The account of Parasuraman et al. gives no place or angle for the needle, and those used here are chosen. How fast the syringe fills is chosen for viewing, and no volume is computed.
- Greene (1935) remains the basis of 44 structures, chiefly the nerves of the limbs and the muscles, and its text has not been read. See [the sources of the model](../living-mouse.md).
- When the hands demonstrate finding the organs of a necropsy in a wide window, they left the step of finding a kidney undone in two of four runs, and the cause has not been found. In a window of 758 by 690 pixels every tutorial finished. See [acceptance](../acceptance.md).
- All of this is practice on a simulated animal. It is not a protocol, and it does not replace supervised training under an approved protocol. See [acceptance](../acceptance.md).
