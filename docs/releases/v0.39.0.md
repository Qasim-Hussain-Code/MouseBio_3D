# v0.39.0 a perfusion and an injection into a tail vein

Dissection practice gains two tutorials, nothing lies over the animal any more, and every step of a tutorial now says what is seen when it has been done. The bench draws less until something has been opened.

## Implemented behavior

- **A clear view at the bench:** in a narrow window, the panel of instruments and the card of a tutorial lay over the view and hid the animal. The card and the list of procedures now stand in the panel, the panel stands beside the view at every width and can be hidden, and on a very narrow window it goes under the view. With a tutorial, the instruments shrink to a row of small pictures, and how the animal lies, the views and the settings fold away.
- **Perfuse through the heart:** after Gage et al. 2012 ([doi:10.3791/3564](https://doi.org/10.3791/3564)), a method done in the rat, with its sizes made smaller here. The chest is opened as before. A small cut is made in the left ventricle, the needle is passed in through it, the right atrium is cut as the way out, and a button starts the flow. The heart then stops, and over about forty seconds the liver pales, the lungs turn white, the vessels empty and the nose, ears and paws clear, which the method gives as the signs of a good perfusion. The flow cannot be started before the needle and the way out are ready, and the card says which is missing.
- **Inject into a tail vein:** after Machholz et al. 2012 ([doi:10.3791/2771](https://doi.org/10.3791/2771)). The view turns to the side of the tail, on which a lateral vein runs under the skin. The needle lies at 17 degrees, within the 15 to 20 degrees of the method, and goes slowly along the vein towards the body. With **Steady hand** on it stops once its tip is in the vein, and the vein then blanches along its length, as the method describes. A swab then presses on the place.
- **What is seen:** the steps of every tutorial now say what it looks like here when the step has been done, and each says what can go wrong where the method does.
- **Less to draw before the first cut:** what lies under whole skin cannot be seen, and it is left out at the bench until something has been opened. This makes the bench quicker until the first cut.
- **Demonstrations that do not depend on the speed of the machine:** on a slow or busy machine, a demonstration could end with a step left undone, and which step it was differed from run to run. The hands now wait for the view to come to rest before they press, and after each step they look at what was done and do the step again, up to twice, if it did not come out. What was moved aside is not moved again. A cut can now open as flaps from 4 mm, which the short cut over the skull needed, and only what wanders to the side of the line counts against a cut, since the body rises and falls under it. A kidney is looked for from closer, with the intestine moved further aside. A needle into a muscle under the mark is now aimed at the body of the muscle, where it used to follow the small folds of the skin at the tip.
- **Sources of three bones of the skull:** the vomer, the ethmoid and the lacrimal bones rested on a book about the rat, whose text could not be read. They now rest on sources read for the purpose, and each describes the mouse: Kim et al. 2021 ([doi:10.1038/s41598-021-87260-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-87260-5)), Norwood et al. 2019 ([doi:10.7554/eLife.44278](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.44278)), Martinez-Abadias et al. 2013 ([doi:10.1002/dvdy.23903](https://doi.org/10.1002/dvdy.23903)) and figure 11 of Cook (1965). Their shapes have not changed, and what the shapes leave out is now stated.

## Limits

- A perfusion here is a change of colour over a chosen time, in chosen shades. No flow, pressure or fixative is computed, and it is shortened from the minutes it takes in an animal. The method is in the rat, and its amounts are not those of a mouse.
- The lateral veins of the tail are simulated. The needle goes in on this model only, and the blanching is a pale line drawn along the vein for a fixed distance.
- The tag v0.38.0 reached the repository without a packaged release, because the packaging step refused the new images of the mark and icon. This version allows exactly those three files, each by its checksum, and its packages hold everything in v0.38.0.
- Greene (1935) remains the basis of the other structures that cite it, chiefly nerves and the fore limb. Its text was not read for this project, because the copy found is held for lending only, and those structures have not been checked against its pages. See [the sources of the model](../living-mouse.md).
- All of this is practice on a simulated animal. It is not a protocol, and it does not replace supervised training under an approved protocol. See [acceptance](../acceptance.md).
