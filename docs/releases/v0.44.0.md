# v0.44.0 paws drawn over their bones

A release that draws the fore paws of the living mouse over the measured bones of the scanned paws, so that no bone stands outside its paw, and that corrects, with exact tests built into the model build, every part that stood outside the animal or below the ground. Sitting up to wash no longer draws the belly out. This version was not published: its fore paws were replaced in [v0.45.0](v0.45.0.md) before publication, and the checks of v0.45.0 found further faults in it, which that release corrects.

## Implemented behavior

- **Fore paws drawn over their bones:** in v0.43.1 the sculpted paws left parts of the measured paw bones outside them, by up to 1.2 mm on the right fore paw, 2.3 mm on the left, 1.8 mm at the right ankle and 0.8 mm at the left ankle by the exact test now used. The fore paws are now a layer of 0.2 mm of soft tissue drawn over the bones of the scanned paws, joined to the forearm at the wrist and over the ends of the forearm bones there. Each digit is also covered along its traced line and joined to the palm, so that each paw is one piece. Walking pads after Cook figure 6 lie under the bases and tips of the digits and under the wrist, claws grow from the ends of the digits in the CT, and each pollex carries a nail laid on the skin. The hind paws keep their sculpted shape and hold the bones of the ankle and the ends of the shank bones under the same layer. No vertex of any paw bone now lies outside its paw.
- **Bones at the wrists and ankles:** the CT does not show the joint spaces there. In v0.43.1 bone that the posture moves with the forearm or shank went with the paw: 6,695 and 3,306 voxels at the right and left wrists and 1,800 and 7,159 at the right and left ankles, which the stance carried up to 2.9 mm from the forearm and 3.3 mm from the shank they belong to. Each voxel there now goes with the segment that carries the soft tissue round it. The simulated tarsal bones of the hind paws, which stood beside the bones of the ankle from the CT, are removed, and the simulated metatarsals start inside those bones.
- **Paws on the ground:** each fore paw is turned so that its bones lie as low and flat on the ground as they can, and the bones of each ankle as the tarsus lies. Each wrist and ankle stands where the lowest bone of its limb keeps 0.3 mm above the ground. On the right fore limb that bone is the end of the radius and ulna, so the right wrist stands higher and that paw is turned a further 3.6 degrees, its digits down. In v0.43.1 the bones of the right ankle reached 0.50 mm into the ground and the hind paws 0.07 mm, while the fore paws stood 0.63 mm above it. All four paws now meet the ground, and the build stops if a paw lies more than 0.05 mm from it, if a limb's bones do not rest as set, or if any part lies below the ground.
- **Tail on the ground:** the tail sank up to 0.22 mm into the ground where it came down to it, and towards its tip its underside rose to 0.73 mm above it. It now lies on the ground, with its wider rings touching it.
- **Nothing outside the animal:** every drawn vertex of every structure is tested exactly against the closed outer surfaces of the skin, paws, tail, nose pad and eyes, apart from the parts that lie on or beyond the surface by nature. In v0.43.1, 49 structures had vertices outside, 22 of them by more than 0.1 mm: besides the paw bones, the nerves and arteries of the paws by up to 0.60 mm, the simulated foot bones by 0.48 mm, the simulated lacrimal bones by 0.44 mm, the right humerus by 0.25 mm and the os penis by 0.22 mm. The lacrimal bones, the irises and the lateral veins of the tail are placed inside the surfaces that hold them, and soft and simulated parts that stand outside are moved under the nearest point of the surface, by at most 0.28 mm. Measured bones are not moved; where one comes within 0.1 mm of the skin, the skin is drawn out over it, by at most 0.36 mm. The build now stops if any part stands outside by more than 0.02 mm.
- **Tissue moved under the skin keeps its shape:** tissue that the stance carries outside the skin was moved to the middle of the nearest inside cell of a grid of 0.3 mm, so that points in one cell came together and some triangles were drawn out across the gaps. It is now moved up the slope of the smoothed field of the body, so that neighbouring points move alike. The longest triangle edge fell from 3.0 to 1.7 mm in the small intestine, from 2.2 to 1.6 mm in the peritoneum and from 2.5 to 1.3 mm in the semimembranosus.
- **Tissue of the scanned paws:** traced muscle of the scanned paws, which lay flattened in the scan, is left out below the wrists and ankles as before, and now also a little above them where a paw holds it outside the body and the limbs; other tissue that a paw holds there stays where it is.
- **Vessels and nerves of the fore paws:** the digital vessels and nerves run along the traced digits just under the skin, and the radial and median arteries and the median and ulnar nerves of the forearm end just past the wrist, where those of the paw begin.
- **Sitting up to wash:** after an observation that the mouse looked drawn out and slim as it sat up, the motion was measured on the skin of the stance: the midline of the belly lengthened by 17 percent and some edges of the skin by up to 95 percent. The back now also rounds forwards along its length as the trunk comes up, and both turns grow along the whole trunk. The belly lengthens by about 4 percent, the back shortens by about 17 percent, and no edge of the skin stretches by more than 35 percent.

## Limits

- The soft tissue, pads, claws and nails of the paws are simulated. The thickness of the layer, the size of the pads, the 0.3 mm of room under the bones, the turns of the paws and the heights of the wrists and ankles are estimates and choices, not measurements of a standing animal.
- At the resolution of the CT the small bones of a paw are not separated from each other or wholly from the soft tissue round them, and the hind feet beyond the ankle are not in the images.
- Carrying the scanned tissue into the stance still stretches some triangles to more than 1 mm, and traced fat or muscle that comes to lie between a limb and the body is divided between the two skins: the longest edge is 4.4 mm, in the fat between the left shank and the root of the tail.
- The figures for sitting up are for the turns of the trunk alone, measured on the skin of the stance.
- The living mouse model is now 52,640,812 bytes, with 2,389,437 triangles. The model and its other limits are recorded in [living mouse methods](../living-mouse.md) and [acceptance](../acceptance.md).
