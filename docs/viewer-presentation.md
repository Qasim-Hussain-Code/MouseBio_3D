# Viewer presentation methods

This document records the display conventions of the three-dimensional viewer: lighting, materials, the skin and see-through body surface, selection marking, the inferred display posture, the ground shadow, the default camera pose and automatic rotation. Each is a presentation choice made in `src/viewer`. None changes source geometry, source identifiers, coordinate frames or the numerical content of a reference, and none supplies an anatomical direction, a physical calibration or a measured optical property of tissue.

## Lighting and tone mapping

The scene is lit by an image-based environment (the Three.js `RoomEnvironment` prefiltered by `PMREMGenerator`, intensity 0.7), a hemisphere light (intensity 1.1), a warm key light (intensity 2.4 at display position (-2.5, 4, 5.5)), a cool fill light (0.7 at (3, -1, -3)) and a back rim light (1.1 at (1.5, 2.5, -5)). Positions are expressed in the display frame after the presentation rotation; the display axes carry no anatomical direction, so the lights have no anatomical meaning either.

Output uses the sRGB colour space with Khronos PBR Neutral tone mapping at exposure 1. Neutral tone mapping was chosen over filmic curves because it preserves hue, so declared region colours and the quantitative colour ramp remain recognisable. A rendered pixel still depends on lighting and shading and is not a colorimetric match of the legend.

The WebGL canvas is cleared transparent. The page paints a white radial gradient behind it; the viewer has no other background. An earlier dark background was removed in favour of a single white presentation.

## Surface materials

Surface regions use `MeshPhysicalMaterial` with roughness 0.52, metalness 0, clearcoat 0.22 with clearcoat roughness 0.4, environment intensity 0.9 and double-sided rendering so that virtual sections expose interior faces. These values give a moist, tissue-like specular response for all regions uniformly. They are not measured optical properties and do not vary by tissue type. Line references keep a one-pixel `LineBasicMaterial`.

## Skin and see-through body surface

In both Digimouse references the body envelope region (`digimouse:1`, `digimouse-volume:1`, labelled body envelope or residual tissue) has the specimen's body surface as its outer boundary. When a Digimouse reference opens, and after **Show all**, this region is shown opaque as skin, so that the first view is the external appearance of the specimen. The source specimen is a nude, hairless mouse, so no fur is drawn. The region keeps its source identity: it is the residual tissue label, whose outer surface is the body surface and whose inner surfaces border the segmented organs. No separate skin layer, thickness or texture is derived.

The **Skin** control in the viewer sets the opacity of this region from 0 to 100 percent. Selecting any other region while the skin is opaque (at least 95 percent) makes it see-through, at the reference's see-through opacity of 0.22 for the tetrahedral reference and the manifest default of 0.06 for the volume reference, and a notice reports the change. Opening Living, Experiment or Compare does the same, because their content lies inside the body. Clicking the skin itself keeps it opaque. The Organs preset sets 0.06. While the skin is opaque, labels of regions inside it are not shown; the region list and inspector are unchanged.

The skin uses the same physical material as other regions with a softer response: roughness 0.62, clearcoat 0.08 and a pale sheen (`#ffe2d4`, intensity 0.55, roughness 0.5) at grazing angles. When the skin is translucent it fades where it faces the camera and keeps its silhouette: in the fragment program the alpha is multiplied by `mix(1, f^2.2, 0.72)`, where `f = 1 - |n · v|`, `n` is the shading normal and `v` the view direction. The user-set opacity remains the maximum. These are presentation effects; they do not represent skin thickness, optics or transparency.

## Selection marking

The selected region receives a rim light, `#f3c98c` scaled by `f^3`, added to the lit colour. This replaces the earlier green emissive tint. The region colour, opacity and geometry are unchanged, and the mark carries no biological meaning.

## Display colours

Regions are coloured by a display scheme chosen under **Colours** in Display controls. The default tissue scheme, declared in `src/domain/tissue-palette.ts`, assigns one colour per recognised source label word: ivory for skeletal labels, deep red-brown for liver, crimson for heart, salmon for lungs, dark red for kidneys, dark purple-red for spleen, pale yellow for bladder, yellow-tan for pancreas, warm tan for stomach and intestine, pinkish grey for brain labels with a separate tint for each Digimouse brain subdivision, muscle red for muscle labels, pale grey-blue for eye, yellow-orange for adrenal glands, pale tan for other glands, yellow for nerve labels, red and blue for arterial and venous labels, and pale pink (`#e3b5a4`) for the body envelope, approximating the skin of a hairless mouse. The colours approximate the gross appearance of fresh tissue so that organs are recognisable at a glance. They are not measured colours of any specimen, they encode nothing beyond the label words, and paired labels such as the two kidney regions share one colour. Colours declared by a source figure, such as the hindlimb surfaces with recorded source diffuse values, are kept in every scheme because they belong to that source's record. The source scheme shows the colours written in each manifest. A label without a recognised word keeps its manifest colour or, failing that, a colour for its first system category. Quantitative overlays in Experiment and Compare replace the display colour of mapped regions in either scheme, and the structure list dots follow the active scheme.

## Labels

**Aa** shows the selected region's name beside the centre of its displayed bounds. **All** places a label for every displayed region whose opacity is at least 0.3, plus the selection. Candidates are ordered by selection and then by displayed size, and each is placed at the first of twenty-two fixed offsets around its anchor at which its estimated box neither leaves the viewport nor overlaps an already placed label; a region without such a position receives no label rather than an overlapping one. Leader lines join each anchor to the nearest edge of its box. Placement is recomputed at most about twenty times per second while the view changes. Anchors are display bounds centres, not anatomical centroids, and label text is the source region name; the layout is implemented in `src/viewer/label-layout.ts` without renderer dependencies. The All setting is not written to view files.

## Inferred display posture

The Digimouse references are displayed with their dorsal side up. The source axes remain unverified, so the dorsal direction is inferred from the source labels themselves and recorded in `src/domain/display-pose.ts`. In mid-trunk columns of the 0.1 mm Digimouse label image (rostrocaudal voxel rows 560 to 636, the range of the kidneys), the median position of the vertebral column lies at 0.84 of the body's extent along native z and the kidneys at 0.69, while the heart lies at 0.30 and the bladder at 0.24 within their own columns. The vertebral column and kidneys are dorsal structures and the heart and bladder ventral ones, so native +z is treated as dorsal. Native z equals display z after the presentation rotation about display z, and both Digimouse references share these axes. The micro-CT reference stores its scan in a different native frame: there the kidneys and spleen lie at smaller native y than the heart and bladder, and the trunk bone lies at small native y, so native -y is treated as dorsal; it becomes display +x after the presentation rotation, and the heart lies at larger native z than the bladder, so the default view leans towards display +z. See [micro-CT methods](microct-geometry.md). The asset check `tests/display-pose.test.ts` repeats the Digimouse inference on the shipped surfaces: in both references the kidneys lie at larger native z than the heart and the bladder, and the median height of the trunk skeleton within the kidneys' rostrocaudal range exceeds 0.6 of the body's extent. The inference fixes only which display direction is drawn as up; it does not establish laterality, calibrate dimensions or verify the source axes, and other references keep the earlier unoriented pose.

## Ground shadow

References with an inferred posture are drawn above a soft ground shadow on a plane perpendicular to their dorsal direction. The displayed regions are rendered from below into a 512 by 512 texture as darkness that decreases with height above the lowest displayed point, blurred twice, and shown on a plane 1 percent of the model height below the model, 1.3 times the extent of the displayed bounds, at opacity 0.38. The shadow follows visibility and clipping but not region opacity. It is a presentation cue for posture and contact and carries no measurement; the specimen was imaged postmortem, and the displayed posture is the scanned posture.

## Default pose and automatic rotation

For references with an inferred posture, Reset places the camera at 3.1 display radii from the centred model along the unit direction (0.36, -0.84, 0.42), normalized, a raised lateral view with the dorsal side up, where `radius` is half the diagonal of the displayed bounds; the orbit and automatic rotation then turn about the dorsal direction, like a turntable. Which side of the animal faces the camera is not established, because laterality is unverified. Other references keep the raised three-quarter pose at `radius × (1.35, 1.05, 2.55)` with display y up. The X, Y and Z buttons remain display-axis views. Automatic rotation orbits the current target at the `OrbitControls` speed setting 1.2, about 50 seconds per revolution. It stops at the first pointer interaction, after which the resulting pose is reported once. Rotation state is not written to view files and is unavailable in Compare, where both views share one reported pose. The model is hidden until loading has finished and the camera has been placed.

## Display smoothing of voxel-derived surfaces

The two Digimouse references and their regional detail surfaces are extracted from a 0.1 mm label volume on 0.4, 0.2 or 0.1 mm processing grids, so their source triangles follow the staircase and terraces of the grid. By default the viewer displays a smoothed copy of each of these surfaces, computed in the browser after loading with Taubin's lambda-mu filter (Taubin, 1995, A signal processing approach to fair surface design, SIGGRAPH 95). Each iteration moves every vertex towards the mean of its mesh neighbours by lambda and then away from it by mu; because mu is slightly larger in magnitude than lambda, the surface does not shrink the way plain Laplacian smoothing does. The parameters are declared in `src/domain/display-smoothing.ts` per reference: the coarse tetrahedral surfaces use 10 iterations with lambda 0.5 and mu -0.53; the dense 0.4 mm volume surfaces and their 0.2 and 0.1 mm detail files, whose terraces span about ten vertices, use 40 iterations with lambda 0.6 and mu -0.61; keeping mu only slightly beyond -lambda holds the pass-band gain close to one, so the longer and stronger run changes the mean radius of a test sphere by less than half a percent instead of inflating it. Vertices on open boundary edges and isolated vertices keep their source positions. Bounding boxes and bounding spheres are recomputed for the copy.

The position filter barely changes terraces longer than about ten vertices: for a ripple twenty vertices long one pass of the volume parameters removes less than 0.05 percent of its amplitude. Measured on the actual body surfaces, 40 passes leave a local roughness of 0.061 (volume body) and 0.037 (micro-CT body) mean umbrella offsets per edge length, against 0.179 and 0.124 for the source surfaces; 120 passes reach only 0.046 and 0.026 while moving vertices by up to 1.31 voxels, and an HC-Laplacian variant performed no better per unit displacement. The long terraces are therefore removed from the shading instead of the geometry. After the position filter, area-weighted vertex normals are diffused: each pass replaces a normal by the normalized sum of itself and its mesh neighbours. The volume parameters allow at most 40 passes (`normalIterations`); the coarse tetrahedral parameters use none. Because diffusion reaches about the square root of the passes in edge lengths, the passes for each surface are limited to `floor((t / 4h)^2)`, where t = 3|V|/A is the surface's thickness from its enclosed volume V and area A, and h its mean edge length. Thin or small regions, such as the skeleton, the paired adrenal glands and the eye, therefore keep normals that follow their own curvature and receive no or one pass, organs such as the heart, kidneys and liver receive between 7 and 40, and the body surface of each reference receives the full limit, because its inner organ interfaces would otherwise understate its thickness. The diffused normals change only the shading: vertex positions, silhouettes, picking and measurements are unaffected, and shading near genuinely sharp edges, such as the cut faces at the micro-CT field edges, is rounded. The filter runs in a Web Worker when the browser provides one, otherwise on the page thread; the page shows "Preparing display surfaces" meanwhile.

The source surface stays in memory. **Smooth voxel surfaces** in Display controls switches between the smoothed copy and the source surface; a displayed finer surface is reloaded with the same choice. Picking and the selected-region label anchor use the displayed copy. Schematic route anchors in Living continue to use source vertices. The micro-CT surfaces, extracted on their own 0.28 mm source grid, use the volume parameters; their heart, kidney and bladder copies move no vertex by more than two source voxels. Hindlimb, EMA147 and line references are not smoothed.

Smoothing adds no resolution. A smoothed boundary remains bounded by the processing grid and by the source segmentation, features removed by subsampling are not recovered, and the parameters are display settings without anatomical validation.

The executed asset check (`tests/surface-smoothing-assets.test.ts`) measures the departure of the display copy from the source triangles. For all 17 tetrahedral regions the largest vertex displacement is 0.54 mm (1.35 processing voxels), the largest bounding-box shift 0.36 mm and the largest change of enclosed signed volume 3.28 percent, in the 388-triangle muscle region 15; the whole-body envelope, skeleton, liver and lungs change by less than 0.1 percent. For the volume base heart, liver and lungs the displacements are at most 0.31 mm (0.77 voxels), bounds shift at most 0.11 mm and the enclosed volumes change by 0.19, 0.01 and 0.16 percent; the 0.2 mm adrenal surface changes by 0.25 percent with a 0.15 mm displacement. Residual long terraces remain visible on the largest volume surfaces at close range; removing them entirely would require passes that begin to alter shape. A separate check records that long runs with mu -0.53 inflate low-frequency shape instead of smoothing further, which is why the counts are fixed. These are numerical properties of the display copy, not statements about anatomical size.

## Limitations

These conventions were checked in a headless Chrome session with software WebGL and inspected visually; representative hardware performance remains unmeasured. The shading hooks modify the standard fragment program through `onBeforeCompile`; an unexpected program is left unchanged and the surface renders with plain shading. No lighting, material or pose setting constitutes anatomical review, physical calibration or validation of any model.
