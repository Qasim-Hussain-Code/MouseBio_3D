# Zooming through the layers

The living mouse model opens as it is zoomed. Nothing has to be chosen first: scrolling towards any spot of the outside of the animal brings that spot to the middle of the view and unfolds the layers that lie under it, in the order in which they lie there, and scrolling back closes them. The display adds no anatomy and changes no surface.

## How it works

The height of the view decides everything, so the wheel, a pinch and the plus and minus keys all act the same way, and going back passes through the same states as going in.

When the view has narrowed enough, the surface under the pointer is found and the place opens there. A thick part such as the trunk starts to open at a view about 45 mm high, and a thin one such as a paw at about 18 mm. The line going in runs mostly straight in from the surface at that spot, leaning a little towards the view. It reaches to just short of the midline in the head and trunk, and to about the middle of a limb.

The stops are the structures met on that line, once each, from the outside inwards. They come from the model itself, so they differ from place to place: the flank, the back, a thigh and a paw each have their own list. Each stop opens in its own part of the zoom, and the opening in it grows to nearly fill the view of that moment. An outer layer therefore opens wider than the one under it, stands as a ring round the next, and leaves the frame as the view narrows. Structures that are not on the line but are near it open when the column reaches their depth, so that they do not stand in the way.

Vessels and nerves are not stops and are not cut, as a dissector would keep them. They remain in place through the opened layers. Anything very close to the camera is not drawn, so that a vessel passing the camera does not fill the view.

The centre of the view moves down through the stops while the view narrows, so the camera goes into the opening, and the view turns to look along the line, until the user turns it. Where the view lies wholly within the opening of the outer layer, the coat is no longer drawn.

## What is shown beside the view

The guide on the left names the place and lists the stops from the outside inwards, with the stop being passed marked, and with a short `sim` beside each simulated structure. Vessels and nerves that pass close to the line are listed at their depths, in the colour of their kind, and do not count as stops. Choosing a stop goes to it and selects it. **Play** goes through the zoom at an even pace, and does not stop at the last layer: it goes on down through the tissue to the quarks of a proton. The scale bar is nominal, because the physical calibration of the source is unverified.

After the view has been still for a moment, pointing at the view names what is under the pointer. A click selects what is inside, and a click on the coat opens a new place there. **Names** also names the structures in view on the right, each with a line to a part of it that can be seen.

At the end of the zoom, the same scroll [goes on down](scale-journey.md) in the same place on the screen, into the tissue, cells and atoms of the structure under the pointer, and **Into tissue** does the same at any stop. Scrolling back out returns to the opened place.

The zoom opens the animal only while it is shown whole. With the skin hidden or see-through, a cut through the body or a named place in the Layers tab, the zoom is an ordinary zoom. [A named place](all-layers.md) remains available with a slider, at a fixed size.

## Limits

- This is a display of the model and not a dissection. The order at a spot is the order of surfaces along one line in this model, and how far each is opened is chosen for viewing. Its width, its timing and the depth it reaches are not anatomical planes.
- Surfaces are hollow. Where one is opened, its inner side is drawn in a flat darker colour, which is not a section through tissue. The source photographs and CT can be seen with **Cut through the body**.
- The order inherits every limit of the model. Many structures are simulated from published descriptions and are marked so. Their positions relative to one another have not been reviewed by an anatomist.
- A long thin structure can be met on the line at a spot where a real animal would have a different neighbour. The list is what the model holds, not what a dissection of this spot would show.
- The view has been checked with software rendering in a headless browser. It has not been measured on a graphics card or reviewed by an anatomist.
