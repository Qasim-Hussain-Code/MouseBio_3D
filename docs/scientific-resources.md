# Scientific resources and reuse decisions

Reviewed 25 October 2026. This review supports the initial implementation; it is not an assertion of anatomical completeness or an independent anatomical review. The machine-readable companion is [resource-registry.json](../data/resource-registry.json). Unknown fields remain `null` rather than being inferred from a resource's reputation or availability.

## Initial asset decision

Digimouse is the initial whole-body source. Its institutional [license page](https://neuroimage.usc.edu/neuro/Digimouse_license) explicitly distributes the atlas under CC BY-SA 4.0. The source model and adapted geometry must retain attribution, source links, a description of modifications, and the same license for adapted material. The MIT license for original application code does not replace the anatomical asset license. Free access alone was not used as evidence of redistribution permission.

The selected [tetrahedral archive](https://neuroimage.usc.edu/mouse_atlas/tesselated_atlas.zip) is listed on the institutional [download page](https://neuroimage.usc.edu/neuro/Digimouse_Download). It contains a MATLAB mesh and region assignment documentation. Surface extraction uses the supplied tetrahedra and region labels; missing structures are not reconstructed from assumptions. The asset manifest and preprocessing report record the actual imported labels, byte counts, hashes, and geometry checks.

Digimouse represents a single 28 g normal nude male specimen imaged after death. It is not a population average, a female reference, a developmental series, or a living physiology measurement. The registered atlas has 0.1 mm isotropic voxels. A surface representation does not acquire finer biological resolution when magnified. The original [methods](https://neuroimage.usc.edu/neuro/Digimouse_Data_Acquisition) combine CT, PET, and cryosection imaging; [atlas creation](https://neuroimage.usc.edu/neuro/Digimouse_Creation_of_Atlas) describes semi-automated segmentation with anatomical assistance. The web overview describes more structures than may remain separately labeled in the distributed tetrahedral mesh. Modeled coverage follows the imported mesh, not that broader list.

Reference: Dogdas B, Stout D, Chatziioannou AF, Leahy RM. Digimouse: a 3D whole body mouse atlas from CT and cryosection data. *Physics in Medicine and Biology*. 2007;52:577-587. [doi:10.1088/0031-9155/52/3/003](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9155/52/3/003).

## Regional hindlimb source

[Charles et al. (2016), S1 Figure](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0147669.s002) provides segmented right-hindlimb and pelvic surfaces from one adult female C57BL/6 mouse. The figure is distributed under CC BY 4.0 with author attribution and a record of adaptations. Its 44 source-labelled surfaces include 37 muscle-named regions, one tendon and six bony or aggregate regions. The source paper reports 39 muscles; the discrepancy remains unresolved. Source acquisition spacing does not establish biological surface accuracy, and the model has no accepted registration to Digimouse. See [hindlimb geometry](hindlimb-geometry.md) and the [asset notice](../public/atlas-hindlimb/LICENSE.md).

## Resource control and future imports

Only the selected Digimouse archive is needed for the initial viewer. Before another dataset is acquired, record its individual license, expected compressed and expanded sizes, memory requirements, and intended subset. Avoid retaining redundant CT, PET, cryosection, or volume copies when only the mesh is required. The full project, dependencies, data, intermediates, and caches must remain below 10 GB.

Each future import must preserve original identifiers, units, coordinate transforms, specimen metadata, source version, checksums, processing steps, and the distinction between source labels and reviewed ontology mappings. Independently registered organs from different specimens must not be silently presented as one measured animal. Missing laterality, age, strain, or resolution remains explicit.
