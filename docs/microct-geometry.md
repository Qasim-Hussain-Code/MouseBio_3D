# Micro-CT reference methods

The micro-CT reference adds a second whole-trunk specimen with organs that the Digimouse references do not segment, notably the intestine. It is adapted from one scan of the native dataset of Rosenhain S, Magnuska ZA, Yamoah GG, Al Rawashdeh W, Kiessling F and Gremse F, [A preclinical micro-computed tomography database including 3D whole body organ segmentations](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2018.294), *Scientific Data* 5:180294 (2018). It is a separate reference with its own identifiers and coordinate frame, and it has no accepted registration to either Digimouse reference.

## Source and licence

The dataset is the figshare collection [doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.c.4224377.v1](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.c.4224377.v1). Its native item, figshare item 7058615, is one ZIP archive of 4,495,345,483 bytes. On 2026-11-05 the figshare record of that item declared CC0 1.0; the article is CC BY 4.0. Attribution to the authors is retained in the manifest, the asset notice, `LICENSES.md` and the distribution notices.

Only the scan `1_nativeCTdata/M01_0.25h` was used: mouse M01 at the 0.25 h time point. Its 11 members, the bed, organ and 0.28 mm CT images with their headers and class files, were read from the remote archive with HTTP range requests, so the full archive was not stored. Each member is bound to the CRC-32 in the archive directory and to the SHA-256 of its bytes, listed in `public/atlas-microct/atlas.json` and pinned in `scripts/import_microct.py`. The extracted members stay under the ignored `data/raw/` directory and are not distributed.

The native dataset comprises female BALB/c nu/nu mice from Charles River, scanned at seven time points without CT contrast agent; the source study gave the mice a fluorescent probe. Age and mass are not reported for the selected animal. The scans were acquired on a Tomoscope Duo at 65 kV and reconstructed at 28 micrometres; the selected images are the averaged 0.28 mm level, whose Analyze headers declare 0.28 mm voxels on a 142 by 105 by 200 grid, a field of view of 39.8 by 29.4 by 56.0 mm. The field covers the trunk, so the head, distal limbs and tail lie partly outside it.

## Segmentations

The scan has two organ segmentation files with different class orders, `Organ1` (ten classes) and `Organ2` (eleven classes, adding the trachea). The article reports a second segmentation for inter-user comparison in 35 native scans; it does not designate either file as primary. The reference uses `Organ1` for all regions so that one consistent segmentation is shown. `Organ2` is used only to report agreement: the Dice coefficient of each class between the two files is 0.898 for heart, 0.889 bladder, 0.878 kidneys, 0.841 stomach, 0.833 liver, 0.813 lung, 0.731 intestine, 0.500 muscle, 0.437 bone and 0.328 spleen. The low values for bone and spleen show that the two segmentations differ substantially for those classes, and they are recorded on each region as segmentation uncertainty. According to the article, bone and lung were segmented semi-automatically by thresholds and region growing and the other organs manually, and the muscle class covers part of a thigh only.

The bed segmentation names its five classes only by number. Class 1 is used as the body: it contains 99.24 percent of all `Organ1` organ voxels and its median CT value is 117, a soft-tissue value, whereas the other classes lie outside the organs. This identification is an inference recorded in the manifest as `bodyEvidence`; the region is named Whole body and keeps the source class number as its label. Its outer surface is the body surface within the scanned field; it has no enclosed cavities and is open at the field edges.

## Surface extraction

`scripts/import_microct.py` converts each `Organ1` class and the body class with the binary interface method of the Digimouse volume surfaces (`polygonize` in `scripts/import_volume.py`): consistent marching tetrahedra at the binary half value on the original 0.28 mm grid with one background sample of padding, outward winding and area-weighted normals. Asset coordinates are voxel indices multiplied by 0.00028, in metres. The source class colours are written as the regions' declared colours. No smoothing, decimation, labels, laterality or registration is added. The converter is reproducible: with the extracted members present, `python3 scripts/import_microct.py --verify-existing` regenerates the asset with the same SHA-256.

The asset `public/atlas-microct/rosenhain2018-m01.glb` contains 11 regions, 628,134 vertices and 1,256,732 triangles in 30,166,776 bytes. The body accounts for 472,780 triangles and the bone for 413,800, reflecting their large and irregular surfaces at this grid; the intestine has 111,268 triangles. Region identifiers keep the source file and class index, for example `rosenhain2018-m01:Organ1:5` for the intestine and `rosenhain2018-m01:Bed:1` for the body.

## Display

The viewer displays smoothed copies of these surfaces with the Taubin parameters used for the Digimouse volume reference; `tests/microct-data.test.ts` records that the heart, kidney and bladder copies move no vertex by more than two voxels. The body is the skin of a hairless nude mouse, but because the scanned field cuts it flat at both ends it opens see-through, at its manifest opacity of 0.08, to show the organs; the **Skin** control makes it opaque. The display posture places native -y upward: the kidneys and spleen lie at smaller native y than the heart and bladder, and the median native y of the bone within the kidneys' rostrocaudal range lies below 0.4 of the body's extent there. Native -y is display +x after the presentation rotation, so the orbit turns about display x for this reference. These are display inferences; orientation, laterality and calibration remain unverified. Source groups select the whole reference, the bone, the organs and the three digestive organs.

## Limitations

One female athymic, hairless BALB/c nu/nu mouse does not represent generic, male or population-average anatomy. Ten organ classes do not include vessels, nerves, the brain, glands or most muscles. The two segmentation files disagree for bone and spleen. The field of view truncates the head, limbs and tail. The body class is identified by inference rather than by a source name. Surfaces and numerical checks are not independent anatomical review. The specimen must not be overlaid on Digimouse by matching coordinates or names.
