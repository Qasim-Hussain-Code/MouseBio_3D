# Section image methods

A virtual section of the registered-volume reference can show the source images of the specimen on its cut face. Digimouse distributes colour cryosection photographs and an X-ray CT image of its single 28 g normal nude male mouse, coregistered by the source authors onto the same 380 by 992 by 208 grid of 0.1 mm voxels as the label image from which the reference's surfaces are extracted. A section therefore displays measured image data at the position of the cut, not an interpolated, generated or illustrative tissue appearance. These images are separate layers of the same specimen; no other specimen is involved.

## Source and packaging

The source archives are [cryo_data.zip](https://neuroimage.usc.edu/mouse_atlas/cryo_data.zip) (SHA-256 `c6b40136e06709ad7b5ce1b3496828b1a282b462ca2ec4a5610971af07c73090`), [ct_data.zip](https://neuroimage.usc.edu/mouse_atlas/ct_data.zip) (`5ca9e99a3a4adeb7879bb4d6c8f45e407b7a49bbc599b3936fa46503cf7acb65`) and the existing [atlas.zip](https://neuroimage.usc.edu/mouse_atlas/atlas.zip). Each contains the CC BY-SA 4.0 statement, which the packaged images retain. `scripts/import_section_images.py` keeps the source bytes: the 208 JPEG slices are concatenated without re-encoding (35,654,474 bytes), and the CT and label images keep the raw deflate streams of their ZIP members (19,868,920 and 899,836 bytes). `public/atlas-volume/sections/sections.json` binds each stream to its size and SHA-256, the expanded streams to their SHA-256, and the archives to their checksums. The archive also contains a Windows thumbnail cache, which is not used.

The slice files carry a `.tif` name in the archive but are JPEG streams with 4:2:2 chroma sampling. The importer and the publication check reject any slice with a segment other than the JFIF header, quantization and Huffman tables, the baseline frame and restart interval, so that no metadata or comment segment is distributed.

## Checks

The orientation of the slices is measured, not assumed. `scripts/jpeg_baseline.py`, a standard-library decoder, decodes slices 20, 60, 100, 140 and 180, and their luminance is correlated with the separately distributed greyscale cryostat volume on a 7-voxel lattice. The identity mapping gives correlations from 0.9988 to 0.9997, against at most 0.8582 with native x flipped and at most 0.647 with native y flipped, recorded in the manifest. Slice index is native z, image columns are native x and image rows are native y. For the CT image, the mean value on a sampled lattice is 4.1 in the unlabelled background, 117.8 in the residual tissue label and 223.1 in the skeleton label, which shows that image and labels share the grid. The photographs and labels are checked in the same way. On every third voxel of every eighth slice from slice 10, mean luminance on the 0 to 255 scale is 15 in the heart label, 26 in the spleen, 30 in the lungs, 31 in the liver and 41 in the kidneys, against 87 in the residual tissue label, 90 in the rest of the brain, 90 in the bladder and 97 in the testis. The dark blood-rich tissue therefore falls inside the labels drawn for it, and in the label image only the residual tissue label meets the background. These are consistency checks and do not measure registration accuracy.

## Display

The application loads an image layer only when a section is active with that layer chosen, and verifies the size and SHA-256 of each stream on loading. A worker then expands the streams, downsamples each image by two in native x and y with every source slice kept, and writes a 190 by 496 by 208 RGBA volume, 78 MB in memory. Photographs are downsampled by the browser's image scaling, and CT by box averaging. The alpha channel holds the fraction of source voxels in each texel with a nonzero label, and pixels below one half are discarded, so that the image stops at the labelled body and the embedding medium around the specimen is not shown.

The section plane is a quad across the display bounds, one source voxel (0.1 mm) on the removed side of the clip plane so that cut edges of regions do not show through it. For each pixel the fragment program converts the world position to native asset coordinates with the inverse of the source scene's matrix and samples the volume at `(position / spacing + 0.5) / dimensions`, the voxel-centre convention of the extracted surfaces. Values are shown unlit, without tone mapping or colour conversion, because they are image values and not a lit surface. Choosing an axis with an image active, or **Cut through the specimen**, moves the camera to face the cut, because the section keeps the half at smaller display coordinates. **Open specimen photographs** in the lightweight reference opens the registered-volume reference of the same specimen, because the tetrahedral frame has no accepted relation to this grid. Under the presentation rotation, display X sections are planes of constant native y, display Y of constant native x and display Z of constant native z. The image choice is not written to view files, and images are not available in Compare, where two viewers would each hold a volume.

## Limitations

The photographs show a frozen postmortem specimen at the block face. Their colours are those of frozen tissue under the source imaging, not of living tissue: blood-rich organs such as the heart and liver appear nearly black. The CT values are 8-bit source values without Hounsfield calibration. Coregistration is the source authors' and its accuracy has not been independently verified; voxels at tissue boundaries can mix neighbouring tissue or embedding medium. In-plane display resolution is 0.2 mm and the images add no structure names; the labels remain the 21 source labels. Anatomical directions, laterality and physical calibration remain unverified. A numerical or packaging check is not anatomical review. The images apply only to the registered-volume reference and do not transfer to any other reference.
