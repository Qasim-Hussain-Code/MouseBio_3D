# All layers at a named place

[Zooming](zoom-unfold.md) opens the mouse where the pointer is, and is the usual way in. **Open a named place**, in the **Layers** tab, opens a place chosen by name at a fixed size, with a slider. The living mouse model can be opened at one place so that all its layers are seen at the same time: skin and coat, fat, the skin muscle, outer and inner muscles, the linings of the cavities, bones, organs, vessels and nerves. This view exists alongside the single layers in the layer stack. It adds no anatomy and measures nothing. It is a way of showing the structures of the [living mouse model](living-mouse.md), each of which keeps its own evidence mark.

## How it works

Each layer is cut back by a little less than the layer above it, as in a stepped dissection, so that a ring of each layer can be seen around the next. Vessels and nerves are not cut, as a dissector would keep them, and they run on through the opening. Organs are left whole unless **Cut into organs** is chosen. The removed part of the skin can be folded back about the top of the opening with **Fold the skin back**. The fold is a rigid turn of the same surface and it does not stretch it.

An opening is a band of a chosen radius around a line on the surface of the animal, seen along the direction outwards from the body. It reaches down to just short of the midline in the head and trunk, and to about the middle of a limb. Inner structures are drawn only where the view passes through the opening, as they would be seen through a hole in real skin, so nothing inside can show through the coat beside it. If the skin is made translucent this no longer applies.

**Whole side**, **Head**, **Neck**, **Chest**, **Abdomen**, **Hip**, **Shoulder**, **Fore limb**, **Thigh**, **Shank**, **Fore paw**, **Hind paw** and **Tail** open at places sized from the positions of the bones and organs of this specimen. The side nearer the camera is opened. A click on the outside of the mouse, including a paw, the tail or an ear, opens the layers at that point, with an opening no wider than the part.

## What is shown at a place

The list beside the view names every structure with surface inside the opening, from the outside inwards, with its evidence mark. It comes from the vertices of the structures themselves, so a thin nerve is found as surely as a large muscle. A structure that lies behind others in the present view is shown in a paler colour.

After the view has been still for a moment, the structures in view are recorded by drawing each one off screen in its own flat colour. Pointing at the view then gives the name of what is under the pointer, and a click selects it. The names stand in two columns beside the animal, and each line ends on a part of the structure that can be seen. **Names on the mouse** turns them off. The names are taken from each layer in turn, with the largest area in view first.

## Limits

- The steps and folds are chosen for viewing. They are not anatomical planes and they do not show how a dissection would look in an animal.
- The order of the layers is a display choice following the order given by Cook (1965) and Hummel et al. (1966). A muscle is called outer or inner as a whole, although in the animal the depth of a muscle varies along its length.
- Where a surface is opened, its inner side is drawn in a darker colour. The structures are hollow surfaces, so this is not a section through tissue. Use **Cut through the body** to see the source photographs and CT on a cut.
- The view was checked in software rendering on the production build. It has not been reviewed by an anatomist.
