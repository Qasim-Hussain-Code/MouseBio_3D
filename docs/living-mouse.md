# Living mouse model

The living mouse reference is a constructed model, static in its anatomy, with an [illustrative motion](living-motion.md) shown on it that changes none of its surfaces. It combines measured data from one Digimouse specimen with simulated structures, in a simulated standing posture, so that a complete mouse can be examined layer by layer. It is not a measurement of a living mouse. No dataset known to this project contains every layer of one animal, and where data are missing this model simulates them and says so.

## Evidence classes

Every structure in [the manifest](../public/atlas-living/atlas.json) declares one class, and the interface shows it in the structure list, the details panel and the label on the model. **Show by evidence** in Layers hides or shows a whole class.

| Class | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source label | Labelled by the source authors. The surface is extracted from a smoothed mask of the Digimouse label image. | Heart, lungs, liver, stomach, kidneys, six brain regions. |
| Traced | Found in the coregistered source images by automatic rules in this project. The location is measured, but the classification is not reviewed by an anatomist. | Skeleton from CT, intestines, skeletal muscle, fat, blood-filled vessels, air spaces. |
| Simulated | Constructed from published descriptions. Course, size and shape are estimates and are not measured. | Coat, whiskers, ears, paws, tail, arteries, peripheral nerves, glands, lymph nodes. |

The posture applies to all of them. Publication checks reject a simulated structure whose description does not begin by saying that it is simulated, and any structure without a declared class and basis.

## Sources for simulated anatomy

Simulated structures follow these works, read as descriptions. No figure or text is reproduced.

- Cook MJ. *The Anatomy of the Laboratory Mouse*. Academic Press, 1965; [web edition](https://www.informatics.jax.org/cookbook/) by Mouse Genome Informatics. Its plates cover externals, skeleton, viscera and the circulatory system, for example the order of the aortic arch branches and the paired superior venae cavae (figure 90), and the lengths of the alimentary canal (figure 57).
- Hummel KP, Richardson FL, Fekete E. Anatomy. In: Green EL, ed. *Biology of the Laboratory Mouse*, 2nd ed., 1966; [web edition](https://www.informatics.jax.org/greenbook/). It gives the vertebral formula, the bones of the paws, the lymph nodes and their locations, the salivary glands, and the relation of the inferior vena cava to the aorta. It states that it does not describe the muscular or nervous systems.
- O'Connell GD, Vresilovic EJ, Elliott DM. Comparison of animals used in disc research to human lumbar disc geometry. *Spine* 2007;32(3):328-33. <https://doi.org/10.1097/01.brs.0000253961.40910.c1>. It compares intervertebral discs of the mouse with those of other animals. It is the basis for including discs; no value from it is used, and their size follows the gaps in the CT.
- Fuller JL, Wimer RE. Neural, sensory, and motor functions. In: Green EL, ed. *Biology of the Laboratory Mouse*, 2nd ed., 1966, chapter 32; [web edition](https://www.informatics.jax.org/greenbook/chapters/chapter32.shtml). It states that the lens nearly fills the globe and is almost spherical, and that the cornea covers about half the surface of the eyeball.
- Greene EC. *Anatomy of the Rat*. Transactions of the American Philosophical Society, New Series, vol. 27, 1935. It is now the basis for the nerves of the limbs other than the sciatic nerve and its roots, for the spinal cord, the pituitary, the inner ears and the lymph vessels, for the nerves of the paws, and for the other names of muscles, and it describes the rat, not the mouse. Every structure citing it says so. Its text could not be read for this project, because the available copy is held for lending only, so the structures citing it were placed from the general description of rodent anatomy and have not been checked against its pages. It is the least checked basis in the model, and it should be replaced or checked when a copy is at hand.
- Works on the mouse that particular structures follow, each cited by the structures that rest on it and listed with them on the Sources page of the application. Among them: Reissig et al. 2022 for the twelve cranial nerves of the mouse at 14.5 days of gestation (<https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2022.1006620>); Sleigh et al. 2020 for the dorsal root ganglia, which number eight cervical, thirteen thoracic, five or six lumbar and four sacral, and lie level with the intervertebral discs, the fourth lumbar the largest; this specimen has six lumbar vertebrae (<https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-020-05147-6>); Zhong et al. 2022 for the C5 to T1 roots of the brachial plexus (<https://doi.org/10.1186/s12974-022-02632-6>); Duan et al. 2024 for the stellate ganglion between the seventh cervical and first thoracic vertebrae (<https://doi.org/10.1186/s40001-024-01815-6>); Ning et al. 2022 for the superior cervical ganglion at the bifurcation of the carotid artery (<https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0093-22.2022>); Trevizan-Bau et al. 2024 for the coeliac ganglia (<https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xpro.2024.103036>); Mughrabi et al. 2021 for the vagus nerve in the carotid sheath with the carotid artery and internal jugular vein (<https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.61270>); Olmstead et al. 2015 for the branches of the facial nerve (<https://doi.org/10.3791/52382>); Sakamoto et al. 2017 for the middle ear (<https://doi.org/10.3791/55054>); Jager et al. 2014 for the median nerve (<https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/701682>); Wang et al. 2020 for the ventricles of the brain (<https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.04.007>); Rigaud et al. 2008 for the third and fourth lumbar roots of the sciatic nerve (<https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pain.2008.01.016>); Kim et al. 2021, Norwood et al. 2019 and Martinez-Abadias et al. 2013 for the vomer, ethmoid and lacrimal bones (<https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-87260-5>, <https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.44278>, <https://doi.org/10.1002/dvdy.23903>). The named vessels of the neck, face, fore limb, shank and abdomen follow Cook figures 79 to 85, 91, 97, 98, 100, 101 and 106 to 108, and those of the paws figures 103 to 105 and 108. A course read off a plate is an estimate: the plates show the vessels of one dissection in two dimensions, and the model carries them into the stance.
- Dogdas B, Stout D, Chatziioannou AF, Leahy RM. Digimouse: a 3D whole body mouse atlas from CT and cryosection data. *Physics in Medicine and Biology* 2007;52:577-587. <https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9155/52/3/003>. This is the source of all measured data.

## Measured inputs

`scripts/living/decode_inputs.py` decodes [the packaged section images](section-images.md) into raw volumes outside the project, and `scripts/living/build.ts` builds the model from them with no other input. Surfaces come from surface nets over smoothed fields, on grids of 0.1 to 0.4 mm, so they carry no voxel terraces. Smoothing is part of the geometry here and is not a display option.

The skeleton is an isosurface of the CT within two voxels of the source skeleton label, together with CT bone at the fore paws outside the labelled body, which shows the digit bones that the source label crops. The CT also shows objects beside the animal whose edges are as bright as bone and lie within 2.5 mm of the labelled body: the bed under it, and upright pieces beside the left fore paw and the right knee. Bright CT outside the labelled body is therefore taken for the animal only where it is joined to the labelled body through voxels at the level of soft tissue, and where the piece it belongs to lies in a paw or touches the labelled body by a face with at least one voxel in twenty (`scripts/living/masks.ts`). By this rule 5,816 voxels at the level of bone that no tissue joins to the body, and 5,053 in 17 pieces that only graze it, are left out. `scripts/living/bones.ts` divides it into 99 named bones and groups of bones, whose boundaries are all estimates. Each bone voxel first goes to a coarse group whose course, placed by hand on the joints and the column, is nearest. Within the column, ribs, sternum and scapulae, each voxel then goes to the seed with the shortest path to it through bone, so that a process stays with the vertebra it grows from. Limits between vertebrae are read from this specimen's CT: minima of bone in the vertebral bodies, rib heads meeting the column at those minima from the seventh cervical to the twelfth thoracic vertebra, and gaps between dorsal arches in the neck and sacrum. The CT shows 7 cervical, 13 thoracic, 6 lumbar and 4 sacral vertebrae, 13 pairs of ribs and 5 caudal vertebrae, in agreement with Hummel et al. The long spine of the second thoracic vertebra supports the numbering. The CT shows no sutures, so the 12 bones and paired bones of the skull are divided by position, with proportions read off Cook figures 11 to 13. Radius and ulna, tibia and fibula, and the bones of each paw are not separated. Limb bones move as rigid pieces about their joints. The CT does not show the joint spaces at the wrists and ankles, so there a bone voxel goes with the limb segment that the posture moves it with, by the rule that carries the soft tissue round it; the end of a forearm or shank bone is then not carried away with the paw. A shank is taken to end at the plane through its ankle across its line, and the bone beyond that plane, within 3.4 mm of the ankle, is taken for the foot. In the scan the left foot lies across the end of its shank, with the heel on one side of the ankle and the toes on the other, so that the heel lies beside the end of the shank; it is bone of the foot and moves with it. The bones of the paws keep the full grid of the CT, as those of the trunk and head do, so that the tips of the digits and the pollex are kept, and specks of under 0.1 mm3, which cannot be placed, are left out. At this resolution the CT does not separate the small bones of a paw from each other or wholly from the soft tissue round them, so the surface of the paw bones is that of the dense core of each scanned paw. At the ankles, before the images end, it is one dense mass whose separate bones and sole the CT does not show. Two small bones lie outside the source skeleton label and are read directly from the CT: a U-shaped bone below the skull, taken as the hyoid, and a tapering bone under the skin of the ventral midline, taken as the os penis. Their names rest on shape and position.

## Tracing tissue

The source label image leaves most soft tissue in one residual label. `scripts/living/trace.ts` classifies its voxels by rules on a 3 by 3 by 3 mean of the photograph and CT:

- **Air spaces**: CT value below 12.
- **Blood-filled vessels**: luminance below 34, at least 0.8 mm below the surface, with thin sheets and components under 1.5 mm³ removed. Arteries are mostly empty after death, so these are mainly veins.
- **Intestines**: yellow-green or tan colour within an abdominal cavity mask, which is the source abdominal labels and yellow voxels closed across a 2.8 mm gap, more than 1.2 mm below the surface, grown 0.2 mm to include the wall.
- **Adipose and connective tissue**: CT value below 88, or pale and unsaturated colour.
- **Skeletal muscle**: the rest, outside the abdominal cavity.

The thresholds were chosen by inspecting slices, and the classes have not been reviewed. The stomach and spleen lie at high native x, and with the right-handed axes this makes high x the animal's left. This is an inference from label positions, not a verified laterality.

## Simulated posture

The specimen was frozen and scanned spread flat, with the fore limbs reaching forward and the hind limbs back. `scripts/living/rig.ts` holds joint positions snapped to bone in the CT. Each limb segment receives one rigid rotation about its joint, which preserves its measured length, and the distal segments are pitched so that all paws reach a common ground. The bones of each paw, which move as one piece, rest on that ground. Each fore paw is turned from its scanned orientation so that its bones lie as low and flat on the ground as they can, which brings its digits down to it. The bones of each ankle are turned so that their longest extent lies along the foot and their shortest upright, with their bulk below the ankle and further forward than back, as the tarsus lies. Each wrist and ankle then stands where the lowest bone of its limb, on the drawn surfaces of the bones, keeps 0.3 mm of room for the pads above the ground, and the build stops if a limb or a paw does not. On the right fore limb that bone is the end of the radius and ulna, a little behind the wrist, which in the stance reaches about 0.4 mm lower than the bones of the paw would at rest; that wrist therefore stands higher, and the paw is turned a further 3.6 degrees about it, its digits down, until its bones also come to 0.3 mm above the ground. The whole turn of the right fore paw from the scan is about 10 degrees, and that of the left about 12. The bones of the left fore paw reach further below its wrist than those of the right, 2.2 mm against 0.7 mm, so its wrist stands higher, and its digits lay straight in the scan where those of the right lay bent. The rump is tucked by 24 degrees and the head lifted by 16 degrees, about pivots on the vertebral column, so that the back rounds as in a standing mouse. The ground lies 7.2 mm below the scanned table and the thigh is held nearly level, so that the knee stays high against the flank and the ankle comes to lie under the hip. These angles and this height were chosen by eye and are not measured from any animal.

Each skeleton voxel is assigned to the limb segment that its bone, as drawn, moves with, organ labels are assigned to the trunk, and a breadth-first search through the body carries that assignment to all soft tissue. A thigh carries only the tissue within reach of its femur, 6 mm at the hip falling to 3.4 mm at the knee, which is an estimate. In the scan the thighs lie spread against the flanks, and the nearest bone to the wall of the flank in front of a knee is the femur, as far as 12.9 mm from it on the right and 11.0 mm on the left; turned forwards with the femur, that wall leaves a groove along the flank. The tissue nearest a femur but beyond its reach, 33 percent of it on the right and 38 percent on the left, therefore stays with the trunk. Reaches from 4.4 mm and 2 mm to 6.6 mm and 4 mm give the same form of both flanks. Surfaces are deformed vertex by vertex, blending the rigid transforms with dual quaternions across smoothed weights. A forearm and a paw are free of the trunk and the head in the animal, but in the scan the forearms lie stretched forwards against the head and the neck. Smoothed weights alone carried the tissue of the cheek and neck that touched a forearm part of the way after it, as far as 13 mm, which left a knob of skin about 3 mm long under the left side of the neck, and they held the tissue of the forearm back. Where the weights of the trunk, the head or the rump meet those of a forearm or a paw, the stronger side therefore keeps the tissue whole and the other takes no part in its movement, so that the two are parted along the surface where they are equal (`weightsAt` in `scripts/living/posture.ts`). The upper arm, the thigh and the shank join both and blend as before; a shank is not parted from the trunk, because the skin of the thigh and the flank joins them. Traced fat is not drawn within 0.45 mm of a parting where each side has at least one hundredth of the movement: the tissue traced there, 17,416 voxels, joined two parts that only touched, and a surface through it would be stretched from the one to the other. Where a parting ends inside a tissue the two sides move nearly alike. The build records every triangle with corners on both sides of a parting, 1,081 in 24 surfaces, and stops if the longest edge of one is more than 0.5 mm longer than in the scan; the most is 0.12 mm, in the biceps brachii. Organs therefore bend a little across the lumbar spine, and all positions in this reference differ from the scanned frame. It has no accepted registration to the other references, which keep their scanned posture.

## Simulated exterior

The body surface carries the source voxels into the stance as one closed surface. The scanned shanks lay pressed flat and the scanned forearms end in a blunt mass of tissue round the wrists, so both are replaced by tapered shapes whose radii are the 80th percentile of the distance of limb tissue from the limb axis, limited to the radii of a slender limb. The tissue that the stance carries beside a limb that was taken away is ragged, and on the grid of 0.3 mm the line where a simulated limb met the body was drawn as a row of teeth. Where such a limb meets the body the field of the surface is therefore smoothed once more, over 0.6 mm: wholly where the limb and the body touch, and less and less up to 1.5 mm from the limb along the body and 1 mm from the body along the limb. The normals of the skin are those of its triangles. The fore paws are sculpted round the bones of the scanned paws. The palm is a rounded shape that holds the bones of the palm, the start of each digit and the ends of the forearm bones within 2.2 mm of the wrist: the planes that bound them in 320 directions, drawn 0.3 mm beyond them and smoothed over 0.3 mm. Each digit is a tube along the middle of its bones, from where it joins the palm to its tip, as wide as its bones need with 0.15 mm of soft tissue over them and never thinner than 0.28 mm in radius, and its radius changes by at most 0.02 mm for each 0.1 mm along it, so that it has no bulges. Across the digits in the CT, the edge of a digit against air lies about 0.13 mm outside the level the build takes for bone. The digits join the palm smoothly, meet in a small join where they touch, as the bent digits of the right paw do at their tips, and are joined to their neighbours by a short web at their starts. Walking pads after Cook figure 6 lie under the tip of each digit, between the starts of the digits and at the base of the palm; a pad whose place on the sole lies within 0.4 mm of the ground reaches it and is flattened there, and the others are low cushions under the sole. Each paw is cut flat at the ground after its surface is smoothed, so that the smoothing does not lift it off. Claws grow from the ends of the digits in the CT, and the pollex carries a nail laid on the skin; the left pollex does not stand apart from the palm in the CT, so it and its nail are placed as on the right. A short bright piece beside the fifth digit of the left paw is the edge of an object beside the animal, which no tissue joins to the paw, and is not a digit. The hind paws are sculpted after Cook figure 7, with five digits, because the images end at the hind feet. Their heels hold the bones of the ankle from the CT, and the ends of the shank bones within 1 mm of the ankle, in rounded shapes of the same kind, and their ankles rise along the line of the shank. Each paw is one closed surface, and the build stops if a paw is in more than one piece, has a tunnel through it or an edge that does not lie between two triangles, or leaves outside it a vertex of the bones it is drawn round. Their metatarsals and phalanges are simulated shafts that start inside the bones of the ankle, which stand for the tarsus. Every paw meets the ground, and the build stops if one lies more than 0.05 mm from it. Below each wrist and ankle, soft tissue that the stance carries outside the body, the limbs and the skin is kept inside the paw: a thin vessel or nerve is drawn in just under the paw's surface, and the traced muscle of the scanned paws, which lay flattened in the scan and which the paws of the stance cannot hold in that shape, is left out, as is traced muscle a little above the wrist or ankle that lies within a paw and outside the body and the limbs. Other tissue that a paw holds there, as over the ends of the forearm and shank bones, stays where it is. Points more than 4 mm from every paw are left where they are. The digital vessels and nerves of the fore paws run along the traced digits just under the skin, and those of the forearm end just past the wrist, where they begin. Whisker groups follow Cook figure 5, with counts and lengths chosen by eye; each whisker tapers to a fine point and curves back along the face. The pinnae follow Cook figures 4 and 5, with a dark opening low on the front of a cupped bowl. The nose pad is sculpted with nostrils and a groove down its middle, and a mouth line is drawn on the outer surface of the labels as in figure 5, because the images do not separate the lips. The eyes are placed where a ray from the source eye label leaves the head. The tail follows the caudal vertebrae measured in the CT, then falls to the ground and lies on it, with its wider rings touching the ground, and its course and length are estimates.

Nothing that lies inside the animal stands outside its outer surface: the skin, the paws, the tail, the nose pad and the globes of the eyes. Every drawn vertex of every other structure is tested against these closed surfaces exactly, by the parity of the triangles that a ray from it crosses and by its distance to the nearest triangle, apart from the parts that lie on the surface or beyond it by nature: the pinnae, lips, whiskers, claws, openings, eyelids and corneas. Tissue that the stance carries outside the skin is first moved to 0.15 mm under it, up the slope of the smoothed field of the body whose level is the skin, so that neighbouring points move alike and a surface keeps its order instead of gathering on one place. Soft and simulated parts that then stand outside, or less than 0.04 mm inside, are moved to that depth under the nearest point of the surface, by at most 0.21 mm. Measured bones are not moved. Where one comes within 0.1 mm of the skin, the skin is drawn out over it, by at most 0.33 mm in all, over the tip of the os penis, in passes until no bone comes that close. Where two sheets of a surface drawn from a field would meet along an edge, as where a limb just touches the trunk, the sheets are parted, so that the skin, the paws and the nose pad are closed surfaces whose every edge lies between two triangles; the build stops if the skin or a paw is not. The build stops if any part stands outside by more than 0.02 mm, or lies below the ground by more than 0.005 mm. Carrying the scanned tissue into the stance stretches some of its triangles to more than 1 mm, and where traced fat or muscle comes to lie between a limb and the body, outside both, it goes to the nearer skin, so that a few triangles span the space between them: the longest edge is 4.4 mm, in the fat between the left shank and the root of the tail, 2.8 mm in the biceps femoris at the right knee and 2.2 mm in the biceps brachii at the left elbow.

The specimen is a nude mouse, so the coat is wholly simulated. `scripts/living/coat.ts` stores for each vertex of the skin the direction in which hair lies, its relative length and its place on the coat pattern, which follows the general pattern of a wild-type mouse: a darker back, a paler belly with a diffuse border low on the flank, and hair that shortens and turns pale towards the paws. `src/viewer/fur.ts` draws the coat as individual hairs: about 367,000 roots are scattered over the body in proportion to area, with a fixed seed, and each hair is a tapered ribbon that turns to face the camera and curves along the stored direction, with neighbouring hairs leaning together in small tufts. About 19,000 finer hairs lie on the ears, the backs of the paws and, sparsely, the tail. **Coat** in Display controls chooses agouti, black, albino or none, and the colours of the ears, tail, paws and nose follow the coat, because pigmented mice have darker skin there than albino mice. Hair length, density, width, the pattern and all of these colours are display estimates chosen by eye, and a real coat has many more and much finer hairs.

## Named parts of measured tissue

Some structures keep measured tissue but divide it by estimate, and their marks say so ("Traced, division estimated" or "Source label, division estimated"). `scripts/living/muscles.ts` gives each of 41 muscles a course from origin to insertion, placed by hand on the joints and the vertebral column, and each cell of traced muscle tissue goes to the muscle whose course is nearest relative to its width. Hind limb names follow Charles et al. (2016), which describes the mouse ([doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0147669](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0147669)), and other names follow Greene (1935), which describes the rat. Fine stripes along each course and pale patches at its ends are a display of fibre direction and tendon, not a measurement. The heart label is divided into four chambers, the right lung into the four lobes and the liver into the four lobes named by Cook, and the stomach into two regions, each by the nearest of hand-placed seeds, with internal cavities not modelled. The traced gut is divided by thickness and position into caecum, colon and rectum, and small intestine. The kidneys and testes are separated by side, and these names rest on the same inference of laterality.

## Simulated interior

`scripts/living/internal.ts` places routes and bodies on landmarks measured from the labels and CT, in the scanned frame, and the posture then carries them. Structures of the trunk and head follow only the bends of the column, and they do not follow a limb that lay beside them in the scan. The aorta runs along the ventral side of the vertebral column as measured in the CT, the spinal cord follows the vertebral canal, the ureters join the source kidneys to the source bladder, and the diaphragm lies midway between the source thoracic and abdominal labels. Their radii, and everything not tied to a landmark, are estimates. Teeth are placed in the jaws seen in the CT, whose 8-bit values do not separate teeth from bone. `scripts/living/interiors.ts` builds the insides of organs as nested closed surfaces, which show in a section, by eroding a source label by an estimated distance: the spaces of the heart, with a thick wall to the left ventricle, a thinner one to the right and thin walls to the atria, the renal medulla and pelvis, the lumen and limiting ridge of the stomach, and the lumen of the bladder. The source images do not separate any of these spaces. Placements in the neck and pelvis were compared with colour sections of the specimen and with the CT, which show the midline of the neck, the hyoid, the jugular veins, the pubic bone, the os penis and the anal canal; the trachea is collapsed in this specimen and cannot be followed. The list of structures and the basis of each is in the manifest.

The build stops if more than 0.1 percent of the vertices of any simulated interior lie over half a millimetre outside the labelled body of the specimen in the scanned frame. Vertices nearer the surface are allowed, for openings such as the external auditory meatus.

## Trees inside organs

Bronchi and the branches of the pulmonary, hepatic, portal, renal, cerebral and mesenteric vessels are generated by space colonization (Runions A, Lane B, Prusinkiewicz P. Modeling trees with a space colonization algorithm. *Eurographics Workshop on Natural Phenomena*, 2007): points scattered in the source organ label attract branches that grow from a hand-placed root at the hilum, and radii follow Murray's law from the tips back to the root. The trees fill the measured volumes, but no branch follows a real vessel or airway, and the number of branches is a display limit that is far below the real number. The membranes (pericardium, pleura, meninges and peritoneum) are faint surfaces at an estimated distance from the source labels, and they carry no measured thickness.

## Beyond the data

The specimen has no measured detail below about 0.1 mm. [The zoom below the layers](scale-journey.md) is a representative view of what lies below that scale, reached by zooming on through the model. None of it comes from this specimen and none of it is part of this model.

## Limbs and skin

The scanned shanks and forearms lay pressed flat, so the limb skin there is a simulated shape. It now follows the centre line of the measured bones of each limb in the stance, and it is wide enough to hold 95 percent of their voxels at each of five stations along the limb. Traced muscles of the shanks and forearms are wider than these slender limbs, so their outer parts are drawn in towards the middle of the limb, smoothly, until they lie under the skin. Any other soft part that the posture leaves outside the skin is moved to just under it at the nearest place. Bones are not moved, because they are measured and rigid, so a small part of a bone can still reach the skin. Parts below the wrists and ankles, in the eyes and in the tail are closed by their own surfaces and are left where they are.

## Named vessels and nerves

Vessels, nerves and lymph nodes that were held in groups are now separate structures, so that a place in the body can say which of them lie there. The order of branching of the hind limb arteries, and their relations to the muscles, follow the dissections of Kochi et al. (2013) in the mouse, which also name the lateral saphenous and ischial veins on the outer side of the thigh. Their deep femoral artery varies in origin, and it is drawn from the internal iliac artery, the commonest origin there. The third and fourth lumbar nerves as the main origin of the sciatic nerve follow Rigaud et al. (2008) in the mouse, and its division into tibial, common peroneal and sural branches follows Shields et al. (2003) in the mouse. The other nerves, the tibial arteries and the fore limb follow Greene (1935), which describes the rat. Courses and radii are estimates placed on the measured joints in a frame made for each limb. The arteries of the forearm are not divided into named arteries, because no source for the mouse was checked. The paws hold one simple artery, vein and nerve along each digit, which are not divided into named branches either.

## Limitations

This is one specimen with estimated additions, not a representative or average mouse. Simulated courses can differ from real anatomy in detail, and they have not been compared with any dissection. Traced classes contain errors at tissue boundaries. Named muscles and organ parts have estimated boundaries, and they do not follow fascia or fissures. Physical calibration, anatomical orientation and laterality remain unverified. No part of this model has had independent anatomical review, and it must not be used for measurement.
